# SC-05 — the normal encounter's physician approaches the bedside and stops

Card `tsk_4d39f0beaa5cdcc6`, A-rows **A05**, **A07** and **A08**. Machine evidence index:
[`sc-05.json`](./sc-05.json). This page explains the result; where the two disagree the JSON and the
command exit codes decide.

The card's claim is that the case's own physician leaves the place the case put him, walks a route
checked against the room the runtime can actually see, arrives beside the patient, faces her and
stops — with his feet on the floor and not sliding along it. Six shipped functions answered part of
that wrongly. Five were measured on the unchanged tree before any product file was edited; the sixth
was found only by a browser run, after the first five had landed, and is measured below beside them.

## The six defects this card started from

Measured at `86dc0300` before any product file was edited. None is an import error, an absent file
or a missing report: each is a shipped function returning a wrong answer to a question that ships.
Full transcript: `sc-05/red-baseline.txt` in the evidence store, which carries both the hard-assertion
run and a soft-assertion run that records all five measured values rather than stopping at the first.
Defect 6 is not in that transcript: no offline instrument ran the frame loop, so nothing offline
could see it. It was measured in a browser and is carried here so the list is the whole list.

Defect 2 is the mirror image. The case authors `plantOffsetMeters {x: -1.95, y: 0, z: 1.72}` for the
physician — "starts at the ward doorway" — and `composeSupportedActorWorldPosition` refuses a
standing offset because `"none" is not a frame`. The refusal is correct when nobody names a frame,
and the same contract says what to do about it: "Standing requires a named floor frame."

## The stance constraint, and exactly what it does and does not buy

SC-00 measured the shipped clip at 8.0x and 20.0x over the plant threshold and concluded that "no
locomotion clip this pipeline has produced meets the plant threshold, and SC-05's remedy is a stance
constraint — foot-lock IK or equivalent — not a different clip." It also said what a foot lock is
worth: "A foot-locked stance is pinned to the world by construction and reaches zero slide."

The equivalent built here derives the ground advance from the planted foot. Each frame the stance toe
is measured in world space and the actor slot is translated by whatever puts it back on its anchor,
so the body advances by exactly what the foot allows. That is root motion taken from the foot, which
is what a foot lock IS, and the report says plainly what it makes true by construction and what it
does not:

- **Pinned by construction:** the single stance foot within one contact window.
- **NOT pinned:** double support, window transitions, the terminal turn, and every frame the drive
  is not asking for locomotion.
- **NOT affected at all:** arrival error, settled heading, stopped-observation root travel, swept
  collision and limb integrity. The lock moves the body, so it can only make arrival WORSE.

The speed decision `sc-04.md` handed here — "time-scale the clip by about 1.63, or lower the
executor's constant" — was taken by measuring: the consumer's advance is the clip's own stance-derived
0.676 m/s, read off the running rig, not the shipped `CLINICIAN_WALK_SPEED_MPS` of 1.1.

## An architecture rule and this card's fixed test path collide

The card fixes the behaviour test at `apps/ui-xr/src/the-normal-encounter-physician-approaches-and-stops.test.ts`
and requires SC-00's frozen rubric to be applied to the actual loaded skeleton.
`packages/openclinxr-verification/architecture-rules/src/archunit-tests/workspace-architecture.test.ts`
forbids ANY file under an app's `src/` from importing `tools/openclinxr/evidence/`, which is where the
rubric and its GLB decoders live. `three`'s `GLTFLoader` cannot parse the shipped bytes in node
either, so there is no route by which an app source file can decode that clip.

The resolution is a split, stated rather than evaded, and neither half is a fixture:

- The **behaviour test** drives the production runtime over a real `three.AnimationMixer` playing a
  clip whose stride is known, and asserts the acceptance-contract limits, the eight controls, the
  identity of everything the case authored, and its own measured foot plant against the display-derived
  0.005 m floor.
- **`tools/…/proofs/sc-05/runtime-approach-measurement.ts`** drives the same production runtime with
  the SHIPPED decoded clip and grades every interval with the frozen rubric. This card's verifier
  suite executes it, and `verify.ts` re-grades the retained observation bytes independently.

The collision is recorded for the owner. It is not weakened here and no rule was edited.

### The physician no longer stands in the floor

The first browser run measured 238 of 782 toe readings below the floor plane, 150 of them deeper than
the 0.005 m perceptual floor, deepest 0.01430 m, split walking 62 / settling 39 / arrived 137.

**The term that was wrong.** `animation-loop.ts` composed the standing branch's height as
`slot.root.position.y = slot.baseY + breathing * 0.018` — a rigid +/-18 mm translation of the WHOLE
actor, written to every standing slot on every frame with nothing holding the feet to the floor. The
arrived-phase trace identifies it without ambiguity: both toes in phase, a clean sinusoid 0.0340 m
peak-to-peak at a 5.4 s period. `sin(t * 1.15)` has a period of 5.464 s, and 2 x 0.018 through the
slot scale is 0.0310 m. One term, not an accumulation.

**The floor plane is not at fault.** Its origin measured -8.9e-10 and the toes oscillated ABOUT it
with a mean of +0.005 m. Nothing here is a floor defect.

**Why the corrected value is right, and why it is not a lift.** `sin` has zero mean, so the height
this composes to is `slot.baseY` with or without the term: removing it moves the standing figure
nowhere. What is removed is an oscillation whose lower half has no floor under it. Breathing keeps
the encoding it already had twelve lines below — `scale.y = baseScaleY + breathing * 0.012` — which
expands the body about the humanoid group's origin. That origin is at the feet: the loader writes
`humanoid.position.set(0, effectiveVerticalOffset, 0)` and the floor-band plant resolves the offset
from the mesh's own `Box3`. So the chest and head rise and the toes do not, and a 1.4% stretch is
roughly 24 mm at head height — larger than the 18 mm bob it replaces, so no idle motion is lost.
Measured on the app harness with the defect reproduced, 1440 toe readings over a 14 s approach: 412
below the plane with the bob, **0 below without it**, minimum clearance 8.50 mm. Adding the scale
term alone moved the count by 2 readings, which is what "the origin is at the feet" predicts.

**The stance lock was tried first and is the wrong owner.** It is the natural candidate for a
per-frame term, and two forms were built and measured. A hard pin to the toe's first-contact height
broke the offline instrument: the anchor frame can sit anywhere inside the 0.06 m band, anchoring
near its top held the body about 50 mm high, and the shipped clip's walk interval went from
satisfying `signed-floor-contact` to violating it. A one-sided clamp `max(0, floorY - toeY)` produced
410 sub-floor readings WITH the clamp and 410 WITHOUT it, byte-identical — it never fired. Both fail
for the same reason: the lock is armed only while `phase === "walking"`, and every sub-floor reading
was in the ARRIVED phase. A constraint that cannot fire is not a fix. Both were removed;
`stance-lock.ts` is byte-identical to `682e6709`.

## What is not proved

**Foot-slide is NOT GRADEABLE in the browser**, and the number is the reason. See the browser
section above: 8.00 Hz median, 2.82x max-over-median against SC-00's frozen `maxFrameGapRatio` of 2,
and about 132 Hz needed before a contact window can be identified at the 0.005 m per-frame allowance.
The contact metrics report `not_gradeable` with the measured cadence rather than a verdict, in both
directions — a stream this sparse may not return `satisfied` either. The offline instrument's
foot-slide numbers stand on their own bytes and are not a substitute for a browser verdict.

**The bound walk clip travels backwards.** Measured on the shipped physician: the rig faces +Z — its
toes sit 0.126 m in +Z of the ankle in the rest frame and its left foot sits at +X — while every
stance window of `openclinxr_retarget_walk_formal_cc0` advances the body along body -Z, measured
forward `{x: -0.0122, z: -0.9999}`. The runtime compensates by aligning the slot to the clip's
MEASURED travel direction, which is what makes the foot plant correct and which leaves the
physician's face pointing back down the route while he walks. The fix is a rebind of the asset, whose
bytes are outside this card's write roots. It also roughly doubles the terminal turn, from about 64
degrees to 116.

**The terminal turn does not meet the plant threshold**, with the numbers in the table above.

**The stager itself is not run inside the tools instrument.** `stageStationActors` calls
`loadGeneratedHumanoidIntoActorSlot`, which needs a browser fetch and a 93-field context; the
behaviour test stubs that one function and drives the real stager, and the instrument reproduces what
the stager does to the fourth slot by calling the same production framing function. Both halves are
named; neither is assumed.

**No clinical claim.** Whether `patient_right` at a 0.75 m standoff is a clinically appropriate place
for a physician to stand, and whether this gait is clinically plausible, need qualified review, which
has not happened. The anthropometric figures the clearance and monitor checks lean on — a 0.3 m
standing footprint, a 1.8 m standing height, a 1.55 m eye height — are external adult floors, not a
validated standard for any procedure.
