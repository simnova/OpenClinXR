# SC-00 — independent controls freeze the measurement rubric before runtime motion is graded

Card `tsk_86cee0308d7f6b2a`, A-row **A08**. Machine evidence index: [`sc-00.json`](./sc-00.json).
This page explains the result; where the two disagree the JSON and the command exit codes decide.

This card owns no product code and changed none. It freezes the numbers SC-03 and SC-05 will be
graded against, before either has run, so those numbers cannot be chosen after seeing what an
implementation happens to produce. That is the whole value of the card, and the section headed
"The shipped walk clip fails this rubric" is where it was tested.

## The failure this card started from

Three measurements on the unchanged tree at `40090435`, before any file in this card existed.
Full log: `sc-00/red-baseline-40090435.log` in the evidence store. None is an import error, an
absent file or a missing report — each is an existing instrument answering a question wrongly.

| # | instrument | input | reported | should have |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `footSlideMeters` | a real stance track lowered 0.30 m **below** the floor | `{slideMeters: 0, contactFrames: 30, worstFrameSlideMeters: 0}` — byte-identical to the planted control | refused a submerged foot as a stance |
| 2 | `footSlideMeters` | the same planted foot on a floor plane at y = 0.15 | `contactFrames: 0` | found 30 contacts against the named frame |
| 3 | `planBedsideApproach` | a 0.05 m IV pole 0.30 m lateral, midway between two 0.35 m waypoints | `pathViolations: []`, `arrivesAtTarget: true` | reported the 0.025 m intrusion its swept body makes |

The first two are one defect in both directions, and `proof-contract-v2.md` names it:
`approach-executor.ts` "uses absolute world Y rather than a signed floor-frame distance". A foot
300 mm underground has a low absolute Y, so it passes; a foot resting correctly on a floor at
y = 0.15 has a high one, so it vanishes. `acceptance-v2.md` says plainly that "deeply submerged
feet cannot count as successful stance".

The third is a sampling blind spot with measurable width. With a 0.3 m standing footprint and
0.35 m waypoints, the circles at consecutive waypoints stop covering the segment between them once
the lateral offset passes `sqrt(0.09 - 0.0306) = 0.244 m`. At 0.30 m lateral the pole's nearest
corner sits 0.347 m from each waypoint — outside both — while the swept body passes 0.275 m from
it.

## The frozen rubric

Version `openclinxr.scene-closure-measurement-rubric.v1`, in
`tools/openclinxr/evidence/scene-closure/proofs/sc-00/measurement-rubric.ts`. Seventeen metrics,
every one graded on every measurement; a grade that is missing a metric is refused rather than
counted as a shorter clean sheet.

### Thresholds fixed by the acceptance contract

Not SC-00's to move, and quoted rather than restated.

| threshold | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| `arrivalErrorMaxMeters` | 0.05 m | `acceptance-v2.md`, "horizontal arrival error at most 0.05 m" |
| `settledYawErrorMaxDegrees` | 10 deg | `acceptance-v2.md`, "settled body-heading error at most 10 degrees" |
| `stoppedObservationSeconds` | 2 s | `acceptance-v2.md`, "no resumed root travel during a two-second stopped observation" |

### Thresholds derived here

Each is anchored to something that cannot move when a clip, a body or a placement changes: the
display, external anatomy, or arithmetic. A threshold whose reference moves with the treatment
passes by construction, which is a tautology with units rather than a gate.

**The perceptual floor, 0.005 m, which most of the geometric numbers hang off.**
`agents/rules/MANDATE_VISIBILITY.md` fixes this repository's noticeability limit as "<3 px on
1920 px frame" at "~3.4 m viewer distance", written months before this card for unrelated garment
work. `apps/ui-xr/src/main.ts:2860` builds the capture camera as `PerspectiveCamera(52, 1, ...)`,
so at 3.4 m the frame is `2 x 3.4 x tan(26 deg) = 3.3166 m` wide, one pixel is 1.727 mm and three
are 5.18 mm. Rounded **down** to 5 mm, because rounding a floor up would admit displacement the
derivation calls visible. This is a property of the display. No clip can move it.

| threshold | value | derivation |
|---|---|---|
| `floorContactBandMeters` | 0.06 m | the existing runtime constant `FOOT_CONTACT_HEIGHT_METERS`, unchanged; SC-00 changes only its frame, from absolute world Y to signed height above the selected floor |
| `floorPenetrationMaxMeters` | 0.005 m | the perceptual floor. A separate signed metric from contact, so a submerged foot cannot borrow a contact pass |
| `supportSeparationMaxMeters` | 0.005 m | the perceptual floor. A body further above its support than a viewer can resolve reads as hovering |
| `supportPenetrationMaxMeters` | 0.005 m | the perceptual floor, signed the other way. Separate from separation because a mean cancels a body half-sunk at the hips and half-floating at the shoulders |
| `footSlideWorstFrameMaxMeters` | 0.005 m | the perceptual floor. A pop is a single-frame displacement |
| `footRollAllowancePerContactWindowMeters` | 0.0157 m | external anatomy. A correctly planted toe marker still rolls about the heel from heel-strike to foot-flat, bounded by `footLength x (1 - cos strikeAngle) = 0.26 m x (1 - cos 20 deg)`. Adult foot length and the ~20 deg strike angle are external anthropometric and gait figures. Total slide is allowed this much per observed contact window, so the allowance scales with what the clip does without being derived from how far the foot actually slid |
| `limbLengthDriftMaxMeters` | 0.001 m | arithmetic. glTF accessors are float32 (~1e-7 relative) and a four-deep FK chain in float64 accumulates far under 1e-5 m on a 0.4 m bone. Two orders above that noise, five times below the perceptual floor. Measured on the shipped walk: 0.000001 m |
| `sweptBodyRadiusMeters` | 0.3 m | `STANDING_FOOTPRINT_RADIUS_METERS`, half a ~0.6 m adult shoulder breadth, already recorded in `bedside-clearance.ts` and predating this card |
| `sweptBodyHeightMeters` | 1.8 m | `STANDING_BODY_HEIGHT_METERS`, same source. Gates the vertical band so a ceiling fixture is not called a blockage |
| `sweptSampleSpacingMaxMeters` | 0.04 m | the step the rubric itself resweeps at. The narrowest catalogue obstacle is an IV pole at ~0.05 m, and `bedside-clearance.ts` already steps at 0.04 m for its corridor check. A sweep finer than the thinnest thing it must find cannot step over one |
| `stoppedRootTravelMaxMeters` | 0.005 m | the perceptual floor. The contract fixes the two-second window but not what counts as stopped |
| `minContactFramesPerFoot` | 3 frames | information-theoretic, not a tolerance. Slide is a first difference; telling a drift from a pop needs a second difference, which needs three consecutive in-contact samples |
| `maxFrameGapRatio` | 2.0 | a uniform sampler's interval varies only by float rounding — measured on the shipped clip it is 41.667 ms on every frame. Twice the median is the smallest ratio a uniform sampler cannot reach |
| `floorNormalToleranceDegrees` | 1 deg | inside the float noise of a normalized axis and far outside any real slope, so a plane the producer meant as flat passes and a ramp is refused |
| `minSkinnedBodies`, `minSkinnedVertexSamples` | 1, 1 | not tolerances. Zero is the measurement saying it observed nothing |

The provenance is live, not copied. The named behaviour test asserts
`floorContactBandMeters === FOOT_CONTACT_HEIGHT_METERS`, `sweptBodyRadiusMeters ===
STANDING_FOOTPRINT_RADIUS_METERS` and `sweptBodyHeightMeters === STANDING_BODY_HEIGHT_METERS`, so a
drift in either place is a failure rather than a silent migration of the rubric onto a number
nobody re-derived.

## The known-good passes, on real unchanged bytes

A rubric that rejects everything is not a gate. The known-good is not a construction: it is the
shipped physician's `ClinicalIdleConversation` clip on the shipped physician GLB, unmodified.
Ninety frames at a uniform 41.667 ms, both toes at a constant 0.00853 m above the floor, and a
measured total horizontal travel of exactly 0.000000 m. A standing clinician's feet do not move.

| metric | threshold | measured | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| foot-slide, worst frame | 0.005 m | 0.000000 m | the whole allowance |
| foot-slide, total | 0.0157 m (1 window) | 0.000000 m | the whole allowance |
| floor-penetration | 0.005 m | 0.000000 m | the whole allowance |
| signed-floor-contact | >= 3 frames | 90 frames | 87 frames |
| support-contact / support-penetration | 0.005 m | 0.000000 m | the whole allowance |
| limb-integrity | 0.001 m | 0.000000 m | the whole allowance |
| arrival-error | 0.05 m | 0.0121 m | 0.0379 m, 4.1x |
| settled-heading | 10 deg | 3.4 deg | 6.6 deg, 2.9x |
| stopped-observation root travel | 0.005 m | 0.0008 m | 0.0042 m, 6.3x |

Support, route, arrival and settled heading have no shipped producer at this baseline — they are
properties of a placement and a run that SC-03 and SC-05 own. The known-good supplies them from
real measured dimensions (the shipped stretcher deck bounds, the decoded patient body extent
1.7756 m against a 2.35 m deck, the production bedside target and route planner) placed
deterministically. `proof-contract-v2.md` permits exactly this and requires it to be labelled:
"This is calibration, not a claim that the later SC-05 normal workflow exists." **These controls
test the instrument. None of them is evidence that any encounter ran.**

## The shipped walk clip fails this rubric

SC-04 replaced the CONDITIONAL CMU walk with CC0 `Walk_Formal`, measured the replacement as about
three times worse at planting its feet, declared it in `limits.unresolvedDefects` and said SC-00
and SC-05 must recalibrate. Recalibrating did not mean picking a number that admits 0.2704.

Measured here on the **shipped** bytes (`4a6d8a78...`, 11,736,568 B), which are not the bytes
`sc-04.json` graded — that report measured the pre-graft intermediate `659ff9ec...`. Graded at both
advances, because which one applies is SC-05's open decision:

| | at the executor's 1.1 m/s | at the clip's own 0.676 m/s |
|---|---|---|
| `toe1-1.L` worst frame | 0.04008 m — **8.0x over** | 0.05041 m — **10.1x over** |
| `toe1-1.R` worst frame | 0.10018 m — **20.0x over** | 0.08253 m — 16.5x over |
| `toe1-1.L` total (2 windows, allow 0.0314 m) | 0.39084 m — 12.4x over | 0.28408 m — 9.0x over |
| `toe1-1.R` total (3 windows, allow 0.0471 m) | 0.33135 m — 7.0x over | 0.30685 m — 6.5x over |

It fails `foot-slide` and **only** `foot-slide`. The other sixteen metrics are satisfied, including
limb integrity at 0.000001 m across animated legs and floor penetration at 0.000984 m. That is what
makes the one failure legible rather than a general complaint about the asset.

`sc-04.json` derives its ground speed from the clip's own longest stance window. That measures
internal consistency and is a fair thing to report, but a speed fitted to the stance displacement
makes that window's residual slide small by construction. What ships is
`CLINICIAN_WALK_SPEED_MPS`, which moves whether or not the plant works, so it is the advance the
grade of record uses. Both columns are recorded; neither passes.

**The retired CMU clip would also have failed.** `sc-04.json` records its worst single-frame slide
at 0.0181 m, 3.6x the allowance. So no locomotion clip this pipeline has produced meets the plant
threshold, and SC-05's remedy is a stance constraint — foot-lock IK or equivalent — not a different
clip. A foot-locked stance is pinned to the world by construction and reaches zero slide, so the
threshold is achievable rather than utopian; the idle clip already sits at 0.000000 m.

The rubric was not widened. That is a finding for SC-05, recorded in `limits.unresolvedDefects`
and carried as a live control, `shipped-walk-formal-fails-foot-slide`, so it cannot be quietly
softened later.

## The two-sided probe, and the gap it found in this card's own gate

Eight deliberate breaks of the rubric, each reverted. Full log: `sc-00/two-sided-probe.txt`.

| break | named clause that failed |
|---|---|
| `footSlideWorstFrameMaxMeters` 0.005 -> 0.12 | `single-frame-pop-fails expected foot-slide to be fail` |
| penetration measured on absolute world Y again | the RED contrast assertion |
| resweep step 0.04 -> 0.35 | `expected 0.35 to be less than 0.05` |
| a refusal counted as a pass | `unsupported-floor-geometry-refuses must not grade ok` |
| `clipDeclaredPlayed` trusted instead of measured | `clip-flag-without-motion-fails expected clip-motion-observed to be fail` |
| `foot-slide` dropped from the required metric list | the control-coverage assertion |
| arrival cap 0.05 -> 0.2 | `arrival-error-over-cap-fails expected arrival-error to be fail` |
| `limbLengthDriftMaxMeters` 0.001 -> 0.05 | `deformed-limb-fails expected limb-integrity to be fail` |

Restored: 22 tests pass across both files.

**Two of those eight passed on the first run of this probe, and both were defects in this card's
gate rather than in the rubric.** Recorded because they are the failure this card exists to prevent,
found in the card itself.

- Widening `footSlideWorstFrameMaxMeters` 24 times changed nothing observable, because every control
  that fails on worst-frame slide also fails on total slide. The number was load-bearing in the
  rubric and in no assertion. Fixed by adding `single-frame-pop-fails` — one 10 mm step, inside the
  15.7 mm per-window total allowance and outside the 5 mm per-frame one — and by asserting the
  threshold values themselves against their derivations rather than only the behaviour they happen
  to produce.
- Coarsening the resweep step to 0.35 m also changed nothing, by arithmetic accident:
  `Math.ceil(0.35 / 0.35)` is 1 on some route segments and 2 on others, and the segment the obstacle
  control uses is one of the twos, so a coarse sweep still sampled the midpoint. Fixed by asserting
  `sweptSampleSpacingMaxMeters < 0.05` directly against the narrowest catalogue obstacle.

The general lesson, and it applies to SC-03 and SC-05 when they are graded: a behavioural assertion
does not pin a threshold. If a number can be moved without a test failing, it is not frozen.

## The oracle cannot accept its own success flag

The measurement type carries no verdict field. There is no `pass`, no `ok`, no `clearanceOk`. The
single producer-authored assertion the rubric accepts is `clipDeclaredPlayed`, and it exists so the
rubric can contradict it: a still track labelled as a played walk fails `clip-motion-observed`.

One trust boundary remained above that, and it is closed by recomputation. A report says a control
`held`. `verify.ts` resolves the measurement artifact through the owner registry, re-grades every
recorded measurement through this same frozen rubric, and compares. A report claiming
`held: true` beside a measurement that grades otherwise is refused, and so is a report claiming
`held: false` beside one that behaves. `proof-contract-v2.md`: "Never trust a second copy of the
expected value supplied by the same report."

## How SC-07 observes production without injecting actor behaviour

SC-07 must produce the run this rubric grades. The boundary, stated here so it is fixed before that
capture exists:

**Permitted.** Camera positioning; ordinary clicks and repeatable user inputs through the normal
main-UI workflow; reading exposed read-only telemetry; sampling joint world transforms, skinned
vertex counts, support instance ids and timestamps off the running scene.

**Forbidden, and each of these has a metric that catches it.**

| injection | caught by |
|---|---|
| writing a transform, root position or heading | `foot-slide` and `stopped-observation` grade the composed world track, not a reported pose |
| calling the approach executor directly instead of activating the normal encounter | not detectable from the samples alone; SC-05's contract owns it, and SC-07 must record the activation path |
| marking readiness, or injecting a plan or a locomotion global | `swept-collision` recomputes the sweep from the route's own polyline; a supplied clear flag is not read |
| setting `clipPlayed` without motion | `clip-motion-observed` |

**Two facts every capture must carry**, because the rubric cannot infer them: the ground advance
the consumer applied, in m/s with its heading, and the identity of the selected floor frame. Both
are inputs to the metrics, and a capture that omits either cannot be graded — the rubric refuses
rather than defaulting to 1.1 m/s or to world zero.

`sweptSampleSpacingMaxMeters` is the step the rubric resweeps at, not a requirement on the
producer. NOT TESTED: whether the supplied polyline **is** the route walked. A producer that hands
over only the endpoints of a curved path defeats the sweep from outside, and no measurement here
closes that.

## What is not proved

No clinical validity claim. These are engineering checks on geometry and timing; whether a pose or
a gait is clinically appropriate needs qualified review, which has not happened. The anthropometric
figures the rubric leans on (0.3 m footprint, 1.8 m standing height, 0.26 m foot length) are
external adult floors, not a validated standard for any procedure.

No worn-headset evidence. Every measurement here is offline geometry decoded from shipped bytes.

No publication. The perceptual floor is derived from the capture camera's framing, not from any
published capture, and nothing here authorises a Pages push.

## Known refusals at hand-off

`verify.ts` exits non-zero on four requirements, all of them the parked state
`delegation-v2.md` prescribes — "the worker requests review and parks; it does not land itself":

- `implementation.treeClean is not true` and `implementation.changeCommits is empty` — the work is
  deliberately left uncommitted in the worktree for the integrator. `changeCommits` is measured as
  the commits after the accepted dependency baseline `40090435` that touch a frozen scope, which is
  correctly empty; naming the last commit that happened to touch the directory would attribute
  someone else's work to this card, and an earlier build of this report did exactly that.
- `reviews is empty` / `no accepted review by a reviewer distinct from the implementer` — the
  reviewer is the integrator.

With those four fields satisfied in memory and nothing else changed, `verifyReport` returns
`{"ok": true}`. That was checked in a scratch probe and deliberately not written into the report.

Re-run `build-report.ts` after the integration commit and after the reviewer records a decision and
those clear. Everything else the verifier checks passes: 12 required checks, 22 required controls
re-graded from their measurements, 9 artifacts resolved and rehashed through the owner registry,
scope audit clean, run identity clean.

`pnpm docs:drift-check` refuses `sc-00.md` and `sc-00.json` as unregistered Markdown and generated
artifacts. Those registries are protected and outside this card's write roots; the integrator
registers them. Reported, not worked around.
