No, MPFB has never been baked or decimated, and the TRELLIS approach does not transfer directly.

Nothing about appearance was tested. Every number below is geometry and bytes. The Quest triangle budget that motivates this work is stated in the rules file and has not been validated on hardware. The consult with OpenAI stalled at 241 KB with no growth and contributed nothing to these findings; its approach can be folded in if it lands.

The blocking design question is slot contention. MPFB's single normal-map slot is already occupied by a working procedural-bump bake, measured at mean luminance 142.1 across 11 assets against a flat-map value of 142.5, std dev 5.75 to 8.01. That map carries fine skin micro-detail. A high-to-low form bake wants the same slot, so the choice is to replace skin detail with form or combine both into one map.

Setup differences:

| | TRELLIS | MPFB |
|---|---|---|
| decimation | meshopt in `iterate-optimize.ts` | none; grep for decimate/simplify/meshopt returns nothing |
| high-to-low bake | present, with cage | none; `use_selected_to_active = False` at `automate_blender.py:1350` |
| existing normal map | none | all 11 assets |
| TANGENT attribute | added via export_tangents | absent, 11 of 11 |

The TRELLIS win recovered form destroyed by decimation. MPFB never decimates, so baking would exist to enable decimation rather than repair it.

Scale: 11 MPFB humanoids total 980,913 triangles and 135.1 MB; a station staging 3 to 4 actors reaches 225,000 to 300,000 humanoid triangles.

Skinned simplification works on `mpfb-peds-patient-child`. Shipped is 61,068 tris / 10,969,488 bytes; ratio 0.5 gives 31,740 tris and −27.7% bytes; ratio 0.25 gives 23,597 tris and −35.7%. All 17 primitives keep JOINTS_0, and UVs, normals and the normal-texture binding survive.
